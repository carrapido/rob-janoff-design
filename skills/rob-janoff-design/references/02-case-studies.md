# Rob Janoff's Case Studies and Design Patterns (Ch. 8 and Appendix)

When you design an SVG, use these real archetypes that Janoff created or analyzed in the book:

## A. Logos created by Rob Janoff's studio
1. **Crooz (game developer in Tokyo, Japan):**
   - *Brief:* The CEO saw his company as a "water planet".
   - *SVG solution:* A curved water drop that forms the letter "C". Adding a small satellite sphere/drop to one side not only suggested outer space (a planet with its moon) but instantly gave it video-game dynamism.
2. **Pluit City (eco island-city in Jakarta, Indonesia):**
   - *Brief:* Rebranding of a luxury artificial city to attract Asian investors.
   - *SVG solution:* Wavy horizontal lines at the base (water) with rising vertical blocks that reflect a city shining over the sea. A red and gold palette to resonate culturally with the Asian market.
3. **Langone Roma (luxury organic cosmetics in Italy):**
   - *Brief:* An organic skin-care line with a luxurious look for Walter Langone.
   - *SVG solution:* A continuous geometric union of the initials "W" and "L" forming a stylized, minimalist crown above an elegant wordmark.
4. **Hearing Others' Voices (cross-cultural editorial series):**
   - *Brief:* Connect human voices and ideas across history.
   - *SVG solution:* Abstract human profiles facing each other and sharing waves/a flow of dialogue, inspired by the symbolic simplicity of ancient hieroglyphs.
5. **RobJanoff.com (his agency's own identity):**
   - *SVG solution:* The lowercase "r" and "j" merged into a continuous multicolor ribbon (a nod to the Apple rainbow) that symbolizes ongoing collaboration between team and client.
6. **J. Michael Bishop Institute for Cancer Research (Chengdu, China):**
   - *Brief:* A fusion of traditional Chinese herbal medicine with Western biological cancer research.
   - *SVG solution:* An organic leaf/drop shape that holds a Western laboratory flask in its negative space.
7. **Sarraf Strata & Lucas / LitterGram (Australia and United Kingdom):**
   - *Sarraf Strata:* Two interlocking geometric "S" shapes forming a solid architectural block.
   - *LitterGram:* A clean camera/shutter icon that unifies citizen reporting on social media.

## B. Janoff's Classic References (Ch. 6 and 8)
- **CBS Eye (1951):** A crisp image a child can understand ("Here I am watching TV and here's an eye watching me").
- **FedEx (Lindon Leader):** It did the exact opposite of UPS (white trucks with a huge logo instead of brown ones with a small gold logo). Five solid letters set together, where the join between the "E" and the "x" creates a secret arrow in the negative space that explains the whole service (getting a package from here to there).
- **I Love NY (Milton Glaser):** A pioneering use of a symbol/emoji as emotional visual shorthand.
- **Janoff's design heroes:** Saul Bass, Paul Rand and Milton Glaser, for their symbolic simplicity (saying as much as possible with the simplest image).
