# Rob Janoff's 5 Golden Rules and Validation Questionnaire (Ch. 9)

## 1. Simplicity ("When in doubt, leave it out")
- Clients often arrive "with laundry lists of elements the logo 'must have'" and obsessed with literalness. "That is a recipe for failure."
- Young designers want to put all their technical talent into a single piece; it is usually too much.
- The message must be "visualized metaphorically, perhaps subliminally, certainly not literally".
- When a dominant icon becomes strong and simple enough, it can stand alone without a wordmark (no words or translations needed for the global market).

## 2. "Do Your Homework" (Research and Empathy)
- "Sometimes houses get a stale odor, but the owners don't notice." A brand can sour the same way. Your job as an outside observer is to discover how the owners really want to be seen, and to have more insight into their business and their customers than they do.

## 3. Don't Get Distracted by Passing Trends (Typography and Style)
- Trendy styles age fast and distract from the core message. Design for the long term: refreshing is better than a full rebrand.
- **Janoff's typography law:** "Many fonts fall in and out of fashion." Avoid anything that might date or look so flashy or interesting that it takes attention away from the image. "I think typography is best when you feel it, but it doesn't get in the way—it affects you, but you don't consciously see it." (Janoff names the futuristic typeface he first used for Apple in 1977 as one of his own mistakes.)

## 4. Keep the Balance and the Life in Its Environment
- Never evaluate a logo in isolation, in the abstract: picture it in a web page layout, on a sign or on a screen.
- **Visual flow:** People read from the top-left corner to the bottom-right; structure proportions and visual weight to follow that path.
- **The surrounding space matters:** "Even the space around your logo helps hold it together." Play with space, proportion, weight and color until the design feels **inevitable**.

## 5. "A Wink and a Smile" (Engagement and Interaction)
- Key validation question: **Is this logo interactive even when it is NOT on an interactive device?**
- Any design that invites the viewer to "look inside it" and discover a wink ("Here's something just between you and me!") creates a personal relationship that is impossible to forget.

## 6. Post-Design Reflection Test (Pages 115–117)
Before calling a design finished, put the proposals through these questions from the book:
1. What story does this symbol tell without words, and what associations does it trigger in different people?
2. Of the options generated, which one would you choose as the designer, and which one would the client choose based on their business goals? Why?
