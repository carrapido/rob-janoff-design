# The Creation of the Apple Logo (Chapters 1–5)

## 1. The Anti-Example: Ron Wayne's Engraving (Ch. 1 and 2)
- Apple's first logo was a medieval-engraving-style ink drawing of Isaac Newton under a tree, with a Wordsworth quote on banners.
- **Why it failed, according to Janoff:** It looked to the past instead of the future, it was a historical illustration rather than a logo, it was the opposite of "user-friendly", and the viewer's eyes did not know where to go.
- **Derived rule:** A logo is a marketing tool; it must be simple, memorable and striking, and stand out immediately. Steve Jobs's only brief: "Don't make it cute."

## 2. The Mechanical vs. Organic Dichotomy (Ch. 3)
- Naming "a cold box of circuitry" after a living fruit ("Apple") created a brilliant contradiction.
- **Empathy approach:** Janoff knew nothing about computers and turned that into an advantage, because he was designing for consumers who did not know about computers either. The goal was to remove the fear of the machine and show "a living-on-your-kitchen-table computer, your own Apple friend".

## 3. From Real Observation to the Essential Cut (Ch. 3)
1. **Direct observation:** He bought bags of real apples and drew them from every angle and perspective, looking for the "essential apple".
2. **The round-silhouette problem:** Simplifying the shape with stem and leaf produced a nice but ordinary silhouette; it could be mistaken for a cherry or any round fruit. There was "nothing arresting" about it.
3. **Cutting to see inside:** He bought another bag and sliced the apples in half vertically. There he found the perfect graphic silhouette, with its top and bottom curves ("shoulders and feet").
4. **The human experience (The Bite):** Something unique was still missing. He thought from human experience: "It's an apple, so, of course, you eat it. Yes, but first, you—… You take a bite."

## 4. What "The Bite" and Color Solved (Ch. 4 and 5)
- **Scale (a "size relater"):** You cannot take a big bite out of a cherry. The bite fixed the fruit's real size, making it unmistakably an apple.
- **Interaction:** It invited the viewer into the picture: "you, too, can take a bite of the new world that Apple computers was bringing into your home."
- **Serendipity and wit ("byte"):** The wink with the computer word *byte* sealed the design's memorable humor.
- **Color tied to the USP (Ch. 4 and 5):** The Apple II's unique selling proposition was showing color graphics on the home TV, something no other computer did; the competition was monochrome and corporate blue, like IBM. The 6 color stripes (green, yellow, orange, red, purple, blue) came from the test bars of color monitors and from the psychedelic pop art of the 60s and 70s (Peter Max, *Yellow Submarine*), giving a warm family and school feel.
- **The icon's evolution (Ch. 7):** When color stopped being a differentiator in 1998, the logo became monochrome (translucent, then Aqua, chrome and finally flat black/white). The key lesson: **color and rendering adapt to the times to refresh the brand, but a perfect silhouette never needs to be replaced.**
